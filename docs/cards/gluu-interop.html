Gluu Interop Demo Card

Bob signs in with a passkey at his firm's Gluu. His agent then uses a SaaS as him: Gluu vouches for Bob, the SaaS's own Gluu issues the access, and the agent never holds either.

Left screen Terminal in the solo-lab checkout
Right screen kagent, signed in as Bob through Gluu https://kagent.sterling.lab

Pre-demo setup

The lab is already up (make up). This card needs two Gluu servers, one per company, registered as in docs/GLUU-INTEROP.md. Done once.

#Run thisWhat it does
1 .env
ENTERPRISE_IDP=gluu,keycloak GLUU_ISSUER=https://<sv-gluu> GLUU_CLIENT_ID=<client> GLUU_CLIENT_SECRET=<secret> RESOURCE_AS=gluu RESOURCE_AS_ISSUER=https://<ledgerline-gluu>
 Gluu becomes Sterling & Vance's enterprise IdP, with S&V's own accounts as the fallback. A second Gluu becomes Ledgerline's authorization server. Two companies, two deployments.
2 Apply it
make layer-45 layer-47 && ./demos/bob/install.sh
 S&V's Keycloak brokers sign-in to Gluu and names Ledgerline's Gluu as the only audience for Bob's ID-JAG. S&V's egress gets its key for private_key_jwt.
3 Browser, once Sign in at kagent as Bob, through Gluu, with his passkey S&V's Keycloak links Bob's Gluu account and keeps his Gluu tokens for the egress.
4 Link and check
./demos/bob/install.sh && make bob-verify
 Ledgerline links its account for Bob to his Gluu identity. The verify run must end with all checks passed, including ID-JAG from gluu.
For Gluu

make interop-keys writes the public keys Ledgerline's Gluu registers: S&V's client key and S&V's IdP keys.

Back to the default

Remove the lines from .env and run step 2 again. Keycloak plays both roles; nothing else changes.

The run-through

kagent on the right and the terminal on the left. Two companies, two Gluus, one agent that never touches a cross-company credential.

#Do thisSay this
1 Browser Open kagent: the login goes to Gluu. Sign in as Bob with his passkey Enterprise IdP Bob's firm runs Gluu as its enterprise IdP. S&V's platform trusts its own broker, which trusts Gluu. If Gluu goes down, sign-in falls back to the firm's own accounts and nothing downstream changes.
2 kagent
What is Ledgerline's view on technology, and which Ledgerline account am I using?
 Cross App Access Another company answers, as its own account for Bob. Gluu vouched for Bob for that one approved connection (an ID-JAG), and Ledgerline's Gluu turned it into a five-minute token of its own. The agent only ever held Bob's S&V token.
3 Terminal
make bob-verify
 Every bypass fails ID-JAG from gluu, checked at S&V's egress. The firm checks what Gluu issued before using it: type, signature, issuer, audience, subject, client. Then the checks try the wrong workload, another user's ID token, and Bob's own token straight at Ledgerline. Each one is refused.
4 Terminal
kubectl --context kind-solo-lab logs -n agentgateway-system -l app=xaa-relay --tail=4
 On the wire Both legs, every claim, no token. The ID-JAG Gluu issued, its claims and the checks on it, then the same jti presented to Ledgerline's Gluu and the token it issued. make interop-logs bundles this trail for the OpenID Foundation interop.
5 Browser Open https://observatory.ops.lab, Topology, Identity view Where it ran Each hop has a named identity. Bob's agent, the egress gateway, the firm's broker and Ledgerline's waypoint all show up with their SPIFFE IDs. The two companies meet only at the edge, the way they would on the internet.
