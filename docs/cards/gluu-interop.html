Gluu Interop Demo Card

Bob signs in with a passkey at his firm's Gluu. His agent then uses another company's service as Bob: Gluu vouches for him, that company's own Gluu issues the access, and the agent never holds either.

Left screen Terminal in the solo-lab checkout
Right screen kagent, signed in as Bob through Gluu https://kagent.sterling.lab

Pre-demo setup

The lab is already up (make up). This card needs two Gluu servers, one per company: the firm's enterprise IdP and Ledgerline's authorization server. Registering them is done once. The steps are in docs/GLUU-INTEROP.md.

#Run thisWhat it does
1 Once: both Gluus in .env
make layer-45 layer-47 && ./demos/bob/install.sh
 With ENTERPRISE_IDP=gluu,keycloak, the GLUU_* settings and RESOURCE_AS=gluu in .env, sign-in moves to the firm's Gluu, with the firm's own accounts as the fallback. Ledgerline's Gluu becomes the only audience for Bob's ID-JAG.
2 Right screen, once Open kagent in a private window. On Gluu's page, sign in as bob with his passkey The firm's Keycloak links Bob's Gluu account by his verified email and keeps his Gluu tokens for the firm's egress.
3 Check
./demos/bob/install.sh && make bob-verify
 Ledgerline links its account for Bob to his Gluu identity. The run must end with all checks passed, including ID-JAG from gluu.
The flow

Every hop on one page, and what is on the wire at the key ones: docs/images/gluu-interop-flow.svg.

Back to the default

Take the Gluu lines out of .env and run step 1 again. The lab goes back to its default IdPs; nothing else changes.

The run-through

kagent on the right and the terminal on the left. Two companies, two Gluus, and an agent that never touches a cross-company credential.

#Do thisSay this
1 Right screen Open kagent in a private window. On Gluu's page, sign in as bob with his passkey Enterprise IdP The firm's enterprise IdP is Gluu. Bob signs in with a passkey, no password. kagent never learns Gluu exists: it trusts the firm's broker, which trusts Gluu. If Gluu goes down, sign-in falls back to the firm's own accounts.
2 kagent
What is Ledgerline's view on technology, and which Ledgerline account am I using?
 Cross App Access Another company answers, as its own account for Bob. The firm's Gluu vouched for Bob for this one approved connection (an ID-JAG), and Ledgerline's Gluu turned it into a five-minute token of its own. There's no consent screen and no shared secret, and the agent only ever held Bob's own firm token.
3 Terminal
make bob-verify
 Every bypass fails ID-JAG from gluu, checked at the firm's egress. The firm checks what Gluu issued before it uses it: type, signature, issuer, audience, subject and client. Then the checks try the wrong workload, another user's ID token, and Bob's own token straight at Ledgerline. Each one is refused.
4 Terminal
kubectl --context kind-solo-lab logs -n agentgateway-system -l app=xaa-relay --tail=4
 On the wire Both requests, every claim, and no tokens. The ID-JAG Gluu issued and the checks on it, then the same ID-JAG, by its jti, presented to Ledgerline's Gluu with the firm's key. make interop-logs bundles this trail for the OpenID Foundation interop.
5 Browser Open https://observatory.ops.lab, then Topology, Identity view Where it ran The agent, the egress gateway, the firm's broker and Ledgerline's waypoint, each with a named identity. The two companies meet only at the edge, the way they would on the internet.
