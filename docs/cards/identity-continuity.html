Identity Continuity Demo Card

Take the firm's identity provider off the network, for real. New sign-ins move to the firm's own accounts in seconds, and nothing that trusts the firm's IdP notices.

Left screen Observatory, Identity Continuity tab https://observatory.ops.lab
Right screen A private browser window, for Bob's sign-ins https://kagent.sterling.lab

Pre-demo setup

The lab is already up (make up). This card needs one upstream IdP: an Auth0 tenant, free tier. Setting up the app takes about ten minutes, once. The steps are in docs/IDENTITY-CONTINUITY.md, under Auth0 setup.

#Run thisWhat it does
1 Once: the Auth0 app's credentials in .env
make layer-47
 With AUTH0_CLIENT_ID and AUTH0_CLIENT_SECRET in .env, this stores them as a Secret. The continuity controller then adds Auth0 to the firm's Keycloak and moves sign-in to it once it's healthy.
2 Check
make status
 Under Sign-in it must read active: auth0, with both tiers healthy.
3 Left screen Sign in to the Observatory as ops / ops-demo, then open Identity Continuity The banner is green: CONNECTED · AUTH0 SIGNING PEOPLE IN. The Observatory has its own admin IdP, so the outage never locks you out of it.
Running it again

Nothing to reset. Restore IdP network ends the outage, and sign-in moves back to Auth0 by itself. If a run was left cut: kubectl --context kind-solo-lab -n sv-egress delete authorizationpolicy continuity-partition-auth0.

Unattended

make continuity-verify runs the same loop (cut, failover, heal, failback, live rule changes) and checks every step.

The run-through

The Observatory on the left, Bob's browser on the right. Cut the network, then watch sign-in keep working.

#Do thisSay this
1 Observatory Point at the map, left to right One issuer Every app trusts one issuer: the firm's Keycloak. Auth0 only authenticates people. The green path shows who is signing people in right now: Auth0, reached through the firm's egress gateway. Below it sits the fallback, the firm's own accounts.
2 Right screen Open kagent in a private window. On Auth0's page, sign in as bob@sterling.lab Brokered Auth0's login page, then kagent, as Bob. He's the same Bob as always, with the same subject and the same advisor groups. Keycloak linked the Auth0 login to his account by his verified email, and kagent never learned that Auth0 exists.
3 Observatory Click Simulate IdP outage Network cut This isn't a flag: it's a real network cut. An Istio DENY on the path to gamb.us.auth0.com, at the firm's egress. The wire reads NETWORK CUT and Auth0 is stamped OUTAGE. The controller isn't told anything; its health checks just start failing.
4 Observatory Watch the banner for about ten seconds Failover Amber, counting failed checks. Then red: FAILOVER ACTIVE. After two failed checks, the controller points Keycloak's login at the firm's own accounts. The live path moves on the map, and the banner says what was cut, by whom and when.
5 Right screen Open a new private window, open kagent, and sign in as bob / bob-demo Still Bob The firm's own login form, and the same Bob. Nobody reconfigured kagent, the gateways or the agents, because they all trust the same issuer. The session from step 2 never noticed. The outage cost a login page, not the business.
6 Terminal
kubectl --context kind-solo-lab -n sv-egress get authorizationpolicy -L continuity.lab.solo.io/tier
 Auditable The whole outage is one policy: continuity-partition-auth0, DENY. It's labelled with the tier it cuts and annotated with who created it. Delete it, and the network is back.
7 Observatory Click Restore IdP network Failback Amber, counting healthy checks, then green again. The controller waits for steady health, three checks in a row, before moving people back. A flapping upstream doesn't bounce users. Failback can also be set to manual.
8 Observatory Point at Transitions and the rule builder on the right Any IdP The sequence was cut, failover, restore, failback, and every step is recorded. The chain is data. Add Okta, Entra or Ping as another tier, reorder the tiers, drain one, or change what counts as down. Every change is live and made as the signed-in admin.
