Pre-demo setup

From cold to ready: two commands, about 30 minutes the first time, and much less after that, because image caches outlive the cluster. One install brings up every demo.

#Run thisWhat it does
1 Once per machine
make machine-setup
 One sudo. It makes *.lab resolve on this machine and trusts the lab CA, which is name-constrained to .lab, so it can't vouch for any real domain. The CA survives rebuilds, so you never do this again.
2 Build the lab
make up && make verify
 Builds kind with Istio ambient, kgateway, agentgateway, kagent + kmcp, Agent Substrate, Keycloak, and observability, then every demo's parties. Each party gets its own namespaces, identities and hostnames. The verify run must end with all checks passed.

Then open https://kagent.sterling.lab on the right screen and start the run-through. Pick agent sv-agents / bob-assistant.

Running it again

make reset rewinds every story: a fresh agent key, empty follow-ups, and Alice's grants and terms back to her defaults. Nothing is rebuilt.

Switching the model
Local, on your GPU (default)
make llm LLM_PROVIDER=ollama
Anthropic (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY in .env)
make llm LLM_PROVIDER=anthropic
OpenAI (OPENAI_API_KEY in .env)
make llm LLM_PROVIDER=openai

The run-through

kagent on the right and the terminal on the left. Bob asks, and the platform decides.

#Do thisSay this
1 Browser Open kagent, then sign in as bob / bob-demo Sign-in The app never sees Bob's password. The kgateway edge runs the login against Sterling & Vance's Keycloak, then hands kagent Bob's access token and ID token. kagent only proves who he is to it.
2 kagent
Who are you acting for, and what does the workspace see? Use whoami.
 Delegated Acting for bob. Issued to mcp-waypoint. Audience bob-workspace. 120 seconds. Bob's own token never reached the tool. The waypoint in front of Bob's tools swapped it (RFC 8693) for a token that only this workspace accepts, for two minutes. Steal it and you get Bob's workspace for two minutes, and nothing else.
3 kagent
List my clients and their stage.
 Two identities Bob's book, and only because both halves checked out. The waypoint allows the call only when the caller's workload is one of the firm's agents (its SPIFFE identity, from the mesh) and the user is an advisor (Bob's token). Either half alone is refused.
4 kagent
Log a follow-up for Marcus Webb: schedule his annual review.
Then Approve in the chat 		Bob approves Reads are automatic. Writes wait for Bob. The agent stops and asks. Nothing is written until Bob says yes, and the entry records who asked (bob) and who carried it out (the waypoint).
5 kagent
Export my whole book, with identifiers.
 Not his to ask For Bob, that tool doesn't exist. export_book is compliance-only, so the waypoint's per-tool policy removes it from the list the agent sees. A prompt can't reach a tool the agent was never shown.
6 kagent
What is Ledgerline's view on technology, and which Ledgerline account am I using?
 Cross App Access Another company answers, as its own account for Bob. Ledgerline is a SaaS Sterling & Vance subscribes to. The firm's IdP issued an ID-JAG for that one approved connection, and Ledgerline turned it into a five-minute token of its own. There's no consent screen and no shared credential, and the agent never held either token.
7 Terminal
make bob-verify
 Every bypass fails Twelve checks, all from real mesh identities. The checks try the right user from the wrong workload, a call with no user token, dialing a pod past the waypoint, skipping the ID token, and walking straight to Ledgerline with Bob's own token. Each one is refused by a different layer.
8 Browser Open https://kiali.ops.lab, then the Traffic Graph Where it ran Agents, waypoint, tools and egress: every hop is mTLS with a named identity. Nothing talks pod to pod across companies. The firm meets Ledgerline only at the edge, the way two companies meet on the internet.
